Title:
Bounding cross-trial temporal coupling in a loophole-free Bell test

Christos Georgitzikis

Abstract (για επικόλληση στη φόρμα):
Bell's theorem admits one loophole that no experiment can close in principle: measurement independence. Retrocausal and time-symmetric models exploit this opening, but the class of models in which the click probability at one trial depends on measurement settings at other trials has been formalised theoretically but never given an experimental bound. We define this class explicitly, derive its observable signature, and bound it using public raw records from the CURBy device-independent randomness beacon -- ten pulses of $1.5\times10^{7}$ trials each, spanning twenty-two months. Each pulse is analysed separately and the ten are combined by inverse-variance weights, never spliced. Scanning every lag $|k|\le 10^{4}$ we find $I(O;S~\mathrm{at~lag}~k)<1.13\times10^{-6}$ bits per trial at family-wise $\alpha=0.05$ (Bonferroni, 40,002 hypotheses) -- smaller by a factor of 362 than the same-trial outcome-setting mutual information measured on the same data. In model parameters, for a coupling kernel of temporal width $\tau$ we exclude coupling strength $\varepsilon$ above $\varepsilon_{\rm excl}(\tau)$, which falls from $7.6\times10^{-2}$ at $\tau=1$ trial to $4.4\times10^{-4}$ at $\tau=10^{4}$ trials for a single pulse, and to $1.9\times10^{-2}$ and $1.2\times10^{-4}$ when all ten pulses are combined by weight. The bound holds for symmetric, future-only, past-only, and one-sided exponential kernels: 208 tests on the single pulse and 208 more on the joint estimate, zero detections in either. For pre-specified kernels within the tested class, the observed sensitivity follows an approximately universal $1/\sqrt{Q}$ scaling, with $Q=\sum_k W(k)^2$; the rigorous exclusion is stated for the four explicit families. Independently, the outcome-outcome mutual information at nonzero lag is bounded below $1/13{,}384$ of the Bell correlation at $k=0$. The absence of cross-trial memory that device-independent randomness and DI-QKD assume is thereby checked empirically on an operating beacon, at the stated sensitivity. The result is null. Its value is that the region is now mapped rather than assumed empty.
